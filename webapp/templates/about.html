Research

Anyone can build a detector.
Knowing whether to trust it is harder.

PhishLens is the demo. The project underneath is PhishDriftBench, a four axis protocol for measuring what phishing detectors do once they leave the test set.

The gap

Scores are measured the easy way

Across eight method papers from 2024 to 2026, none tested over time, at realistic phishing rarity, or against evasion.

The finding

99% accuracy can mean 6% precision

Corrected for how rare phishing is, a detector's alerts can be wrong 16 times out of 17 without its headline accuracy moving.

The response

A model that knows when to stop

Explains each verdict, abstains rather than guessing, and repairs itself using 20 labels rather than thousands.

What it cannot do.

Stating this plainly is the point of the project, not a disclaimer bolted onto it.

  • Follow a shortened link. The destination is not in the URL text, so it abstains.
  • See anything on the page. No HTML, no images, no redirects, by design.
  • Catch a correctly spelled impersonation. paypal-support-team.com is spelled properly. Recognising the intent needs reasoning this model does not do.
  • Promise these numbers on your traffic. Results come from a single training seed on three public corpora.
  • Replace a security stack. One cheap signal before the expensive checks, not instead of them.

Questions.

Does it visit the website I paste in?
No. Only the characters of the URL are analysed. Nothing is requested, rendered or stored, which is what makes it safe to paste a suspected phishing link.
How accurate is it, really?
97.4% on a balanced held out set of 20,000 URLs, and that number is misleading on its own, which is why the Results page exists. At realistic phishing rates, precision falls to single digits.
Why does it sometimes refuse to answer?
Because the URL carries too little evidence. Shorteners hide their destination entirely. Abstaining costs 3.6% coverage and nearly halves the error rate on the rest.
Can I run it myself?
Yes. Code, trained model and all 56 result files are public, with a manifest mapping every number to the script that produced it. Run python webapp/app.py.
